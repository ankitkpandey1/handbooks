No. Cooperation does not reliably survive across the stated ranges.

The ranges permit qualitatively different games. For example:

- \(R=3,\ S=1,\ T=3,\ P=1\): cooperation can be sustained.
- \(R=2.5,\ S=-0.5,\ T=4.5,\ P=1\): \(T>R>P>S\), a Prisoner’s-Dilemma-like game favoring defection.

Because the ranges overlap in the key comparisons, the intervention’s effect is not identified:

- \(R-T\) determines whether cooperating against a cooperator beats defecting against one.
- \(S-P\) determines whether cooperating against a defector beats defecting against one.

These ranges do not provide probabilities, so they cannot support an average predicted survival rate or any invented observed outcome. Also, survival depends on population size, update rule, mutation/noise, and the initial fraction of cooperators.

The most decision-relevant measurement would be a calibrated, model-specific cooperation persistence or fixation probability, reported conditional on:

1. the actual payoff parameters,
2. the evolutionary/update dynamics, and
3. the starting population composition.

For robustness under uncertainty, report the worst-case value over the plausible parameter and starting-state set—not an unweighted average over the ranges.
