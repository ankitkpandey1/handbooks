# E10 Result — No Universal Architecture Winner

**Claim level:** `ASSESSED_INTERNAL` with reproducible schema checks
**Coverage:** 20 tasks, including 7 SWE tasks (35%)

All seven conditions returned 20 valid records with every required field. This establishes execution completeness only.

## Observed distinctions

- A5 evidence compiler was most explicit on provenance-heavy research and the weak-verifier hiring notice.
- A3 options naturally emphasized reversible tests in adaptive strategy and uncertain portfolios.
- A1 control naturally emphasized sensors, thresholds, staged migration, and monitored operations.
- A2 bottleneck was concise on incident localization, reconciliation, and systemic SWE constraint crossing.
- A4 adversarial market generated useful competing proposals for consequential ambiguity but added unjustified generator/falsifier ceremony to the isolated typo.
- A0 lifecycle produced strong general workflows once the mandatory output contract forced selectors, bounds, and learning.
- H did not consistently outperform A0 and sometimes used awkward claim/action phrasing. Its richer definition remains a confound.

## Architecture-level result

The task schema caused substantial convergence. This is substantive: a stable operating contract—binding constraint, first action, scalable work, selector, bounded review, stop/authority, learning—accounts for much of the useful behavior. No single ontology deserves to organize all chapters.

The selected design is therefore:

1. one invariant constraint-crossing spine;
2. a router choosing one conditional operator: evidence compiler, bottleneck demolition, real options, feedback control, or adversarial search;
3. selector-before-scale and typed review objects across all operators;
4. six required action chapters as the learning sequence, not six claims about one universal workflow.

This design is a synthesis from experimental failures, not an outcome-validated winner. A4 is explicitly rejected as a universal architecture. H is rejected as a self-sufficient monolithic architecture. Pure A1/A3/A5 are retained as conditional modules.

## SWE allocation implication

The final five worked demonstrations contain two SWE cases (40%): systemic authorization prevention and safe multi-consumer migration. The remaining research, simulation/strategy, and weak-verifier/early-stop cases protect domain generality.
