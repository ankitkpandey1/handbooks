Scoring key: each pair is scored 0–4 on, in order: **A** exact first action, **W** machine-scale workload, **S** predeclared selector/falsifier, **R** maximum human-reviewed object, **B** stop/authority boundary, **L** durable learning. A nonzero score cites the plan feature in brackets.

“Machine-scale” means automated or batchable work; no outcomes, costs, or measurements are assumed.

## Operational distinguishability from P0

- **P1: Yes.** Its identity is the explicit target–sensor–state–error–policy loop. If those disappear, it has collapsed into P0.
- **P2: Yes.** Its identity is binding-constraint diagnosis plus a deliberately manufactured discriminator and crossing capability.
- **P3: Yes.** Its identity is option value, staged reversible bets, and information pricing.
- **P4: Yes.** Its identity is externalized heterogeneous generation, funded falsification, and reputation/budget updates.
- **P5: Yes.** Its identity is typed claims, explicit evidence graphs, node rejection, and bounded decision objects.
- **P6: Yes, but only if enforced.** It is distinguishable by classifying the constraint across six layers, requiring pre-scaling selectors, typed bounded objects, feedback control, and option staging. Without those, it is mostly P2 + P5 boilerplate.

## Disqualifying failures

A pair is disqualified if it:

- scales work before specifying a selector/falsifier;
- treats duplicated or unsupported evidence as independent support;
- exceeds the stated human-review or experiment/test capacity;
- converts an unresolved uncertainty into an irreversible action;
- grants the analyst or machine authority not granted by the task;
- records no reusable representation of failures, rejected claims, or policy updates;
- uses generic “review evidence and iterate” language without the logic’s defining mechanism.

By those criteria, P0 is disqualified on T1 if dataset dependence is not resolved, on T2 if it recommends launch rather than staging two tests, and on T3 if it produces a final enforcement regime despite weak evidence. P1 is disqualified if it lacks measurable sensors. P2 is disqualified if it names a constraint but does not create a discriminator. P3 is disqualified if options are merely alternatives without exercise criteria. P4 is disqualified if generators are homogeneous or falsifiers are unfunded. P5 is disqualified if the graph is only a bibliography without typed rejection rules. P6 is disqualified if its six-layer diagnosis is decorative or if it skips pre-scaling selection.

## Provisional ranking

**Winner: P6.** It subsumes the strongest operational safeguards: constraint localization, predeclared discrimination, typed bounded outputs, authority boundaries, feedback control, and option staging.

**Runner-up: P5.** It is exceptionally strong where evidence structure and unsupported-claim rejection dominate, especially T1 and T3.

The winner changes only in a task-specific sense:

- **T1:** P5 may be preferred provisionally if the central risk is evidence provenance and dataset reuse; P6 remains stronger if experiment selection and review capacity are the binding constraints.
- **T2:** P3 may outperform P6 for a purely financial/strategic option problem because reversible bets and exercise criteria are primary.
- **T3:** P6 is the clearest winner because authority, enforcement harm, feedback control, and staged policy options all matter simultaneously.

No empirical winner is asserted; these are architectural rankings from the specified first-48-hour plans, not observed performance.
