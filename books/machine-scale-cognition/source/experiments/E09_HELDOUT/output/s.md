Binding constraint: representation.

The conjecture, graph class, invariant, and 120 observations are unspecified. Without them, coverage and discrimination cannot be assessed, and no proof or counterexample search can be meaningfully designed.

Selector before scaling:

> Can the conjecture be written as a precise quantified statement
> \(P(G,\mathcal I(G))\) over a specified graph class, with the 120 observations attached as reproducible instances?

If no, spend the week reconstructing the statement—not scaling computation or attempting a proof.

Bounded object for the mathematician:

1. **Formalize**
   - Define the graph class.
   - Define the invariant, including conventions and edge cases.
   - State the conjecture with all quantifiers.
   - Record each observation as \((G_i,\mathcal I(G_i),\text{claimed relation})\).

2. **Audit the data**
   - Check reproducibility and duplicates.
   - Identify graph sizes and structural regimes covered.
   - Separate observations supporting the conjecture from observations that merely motivated it.
   - Search small omitted cases and boundary cases.

3. **Choose one of three outcomes**
   - **Pursue proof:** the statement survives the audit and has a plausible structural mechanism or reduction.
   - **Find counterexamples:** the statement fails on an omitted or adversarially generated family.
   - **Reformulate:** the pattern holds only with additional hypotheses, altered quantifiers, or a weaker conclusion.

4. **End-of-week deliverable**
   - One precise conjecture (or a documented reason it cannot yet be stated).
   - A checked table of the 120 observations.
   - The smallest counterexample found, if any.
   - One recommended next action with its rationale.

Stop condition: do not claim evidence for, against, or about the truth of the conjecture until the missing mathematical objects are supplied and the statement is formally represented.
